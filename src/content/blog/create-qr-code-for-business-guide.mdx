## Choosing the Right QR Code Type for Your Business

Not all QR codes are created equal. Understanding the difference between static and dynamic codes is crucial for long-term success.

### Static QR Codes
Static QR codes encode the destination data directly into the pattern of the code. This means once generated, the destination cannot be changed. They are ideal for permanent information, such as plain text, a permanent email address, or a static vCard.

### Dynamic QR Codes
Dynamic QR codes use a short redirect URL encoded in the image. This redirect URL points to the final destination, which can be changed at any time without altering the physical QR code. Dynamic codes are essential for marketing campaigns, menus that change frequently, and anytime you need to track scan analytics.

## Best Practices for High-Scannability

A QR code is useless if it cannot be scanned quickly. Adhere to these best practices:

- **High Contrast:** The foreground (the code pattern) must be significantly darker than the background. Black on white is always the safest choice.
- **Maintain the Quiet Zone:** Ensure there is a clear margin (the quiet zone) around the QR code, free of any other graphics or text, so scanners can easily identify the code's boundaries.
- **Appropriate Error Correction:** Use a higher error correction level (like Level H) if the code will be printed in an environment where it might get damaged or dirty, or if you are placing a large logo in the center.
- **Size Matters:** Ensure the code is large enough to be scanned from the expected distance. A general rule of thumb is a 10:1 ratio of distance to size.

## Tracking and Analytics – Measure What Matters

To truly leverage QR codes as a marketing tool, you must track their performance. Key metrics to monitor include:

- **Scan Volume:** Total number of scans over time.
- **Unique vs. Total Scans:** How many individuals scanned the code versus repeated scans by the same person.
- **Location Data:** Where the scans are occurring geographically.
- **Device Types:** Whether users are scanning with iOS or Android devices.

By analyzing this data, you can optimize your campaigns and understand which physical placements are driving the most digital engagement.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced marketers can make mistakes when deploying QR codes. Avoid these common pitfalls:

- **Too Small:** Printing codes smaller than 2x2 cm significantly reduces scannability.
- **Low Contrast:** Using light colors on a light background will confuse scanners.
- **No Call to Action (CTA):** A naked QR code rarely gets scanned. Always include a CTA like "Scan to View Menu" or "Scan for 20% Off."
- **Broken Links:** Failing to verify the destination URL before mass printing can lead to disastrous 404 errors and wasted marketing spend.
